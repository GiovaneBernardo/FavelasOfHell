# ADR 0014: A real district: streets, buildings, beach and props (Leblon / Ipanema)

Status: Proposed, implemented for the first district (2026-10-10). Extends [ADR 0012](0012-world-authoring.md) (terrain,
vegetation, manual roads), which listed "parcels, the building generator, wires, intersections" as next.

## Context

The lead wants Leblon / Ipanema recreated on the real-Rio heightmap: the beach, the streets and their decoration, and the
buildings, with buildings that adapt to the ground and to their neighbours. Two facts shaped the design:

- The Copernicus GLO-30 heightmap is a *surface* model. The plain of Leblon and Ipanema reads 10-15 m (street level is 2-5 m)
  because the buildings are in it, and its coastline is 15-30 m off, so the beach and the avenue could not be made from it.
- Hand-placing about 4,000 street segments and 12,000 buildings is not an option, and the manual spline `Road` tool
  (ADR 0012) has no junctions: roads overlap where they cross.

## Decision

**The district is data, built offline from OpenStreetMap; the engine generates the geometry from it.**

```
tools/fetch_osm_district.py   Overpass -> data/osm/*.json            (git-ignored; ODbL, see world/ATTRIBUTION.md)
tools/build_district.py       data/osm + world/rio_dem.fohdem
                                -> world/district_leblon.fohcity     street graph, footprints, areas, points
                                -> world/leblon_ground.fohp          height patch + surface mask
engine/world  City            chunk streaming, generators          (foh/world/city.h)
              GroundPatch     the finer ground over the heightmap  (foh/world/ground_patch.h)
editor        CityPanel       View > City, flag --city
```

Both files are derived data (the patch is 21 MB): they are git-ignored and regenerated by the two scripts (about 15 s once the
download is cached). A better source (a survey, a cadastre) replaces the files without engine changes.

### Ground (`tools/build_district.py`, `GroundPatch`)

The patch is a 2 m height grid plus a weight over the heightmap: `final = lerp(heightmap + detail, patch, weight)`, so the
heightmap keeps its procedural detail wherever the weight is 0 (hills), and the patch is exact where it matters.

- **Beach and sea.** The sea is found by flooding from open water against the OSM coastline; the berm rises from the waterline
  as `3.15 (1 - exp(-s / 17))` (s = metres inland from the shore) into the promenade, the sea bed falls with Ipanema's real
  slope (about -5 m 120 m out) and joins the heightmap's bed beyond 300-700 m. The Lagoa Rodrigo de Freitas comes from its OSM
  polygon (a shallow bowl, the shore a quay), the Jardim de Alah canal from its way (a 2.6 m trench).
- **The plain.** Surface-model bias is removed with a 62 m erosion (it finds the ground between the blocks) and a smoothing;
  on the hills the surface model is smoothed over 16 m with the bias fading out as the ground rises, so the plain hands over
  to the hills without a step (plain vs hill is the slope of the 60 m smoothed surface: below 3.5 % flat, above 11 % hill).
- **Adapting to the terrain.** Hillside roads get a grade-limited profile level across (the idea of `CarveRoad`, baked), and every
  building whose ground is not owned by the patch gets a **platform**: ground floor at a low percentile of the smoothed ground
  under it, the ground levelled to it inside the footprint + 2 m and blended back over 5 m. The building's `baseZ` is written
  to the file, so the generator and the ground agree.
- **Surface mask** (4 m, RGBA8: paving, sand, lawn). Sand is what is open between the waterline and the first street or
  building (a flood fill from the shore), paving is within 30 m of a building or a street, lawn is parks and flat open land.
  The terrain material reads it (`MaterialParams::terrainMask`, `shaders/opaque.slang`): the city at 3 m is not beach. The
  mask also tells the vegetation where natural plants may grow (`VegetationMask`), so trees and grass keep off paving and sand.

### Streets (`city_roads.cpp`)

The graph is split at every shared node, each edge carries class, lanes, width, sidewalk widths and flags. At every node the
incident streets are sorted by angle, the corner where the kerbs of consecutive streets meet is solved, rounded with a fillet
(1.5-4.5 m by class), and each street is cut back to its corners. That gives real junction polygons, curb arcs and sidewalk
corner patches; a side with no corner (the far side of a T) keeps its sidewalk running. Asphalt (with dashed lane
dividers, double yellow centre lines, edge lines), curbed sidewalks, the black-and-white-wave promenade on the sea side of
the beach avenues (a 7 m sidewalk), cycle paths (red), pedestrian streets, footpaths and zebra crossings at the surveyed
crossings are meshes draped on the terrain. Footways that run alongside a street are dropped (they are its sidewalk).

### Buildings (`city_buildings.cpp`)

One generated mesh per footprint, with the walls as single quads with repeating textures (one texture repeat = one bay of one
storey, so the number of bays is `round(wallLength / 3.6)` and windows always line up with the wall): a ground floor
(per bay: shop front variants on commercial buildings; entrance, window and garage bays on residential ones; only on walls that
face a street), storeys, a parapet band, roof. The neighbours' walls are found in a grid; where a taller or equal neighbour
shares a wall (parallel, facing, within 0.7 m) the shared part is not drawn, and where the neighbour is lower the part above
it is a blank party wall. A concrete foundation runs from the ground floor down to the lowest ground in front of the wall, so
slopes never open a gap (and a platform on a steep hillside reads as a retaining base). Roofs: flat slab with a recessed
parapet, stair/lift housings, antennas and water tanks (hash-placed inside the footprint), or hip roofs with tile texture on
small rectangular houses; canopies (`building=roof`) are slabs on pillars. Heights come from OSM (10.5k of 12.5k buildings have
one); the rest get a plausible one by footprint size. Facades, shop fronts, paving, asphalt, markings and roofs are
**procedural textures** generated at load (`city_assets.cpp`; albedo, normal and metal/roughness; 3 facade styles x 5 palettes,
4 shop variants), so the city looks like a city with no imported asset; an artist's set with the same sizes replaces them.

### Props (`city_props.cpp`)

Street lamps (surveyed and every 30 m), traffic lights, bus shelters, benches, bins; on the beach front lifeguard posts, kiosks,
volleyball courts and ~thousands of umbrellas in clusters on the sand. Instances of shared meshes (colour through the instance
tint). Trees: the 3,100 surveyed trees, street trees along sidewalks and park trees, given to `Vegetation` as explicit plants.

### Streaming

Nothing is an entity. The world is cut into 128 m chunks; chunks within `range` (450 m) are built in full detail on the job
system (low priority), out to `farRange` (1,800 m) as a far version (one quad per wall, flat roof, asphalt only, no shadows) with
hysteresis between the two; uploads are budgeted per frame. A chunk is a handful of merged meshes drawn as retained
`StaticInstanceSet`s (culled per viewport and shadow cascade, like vegetation). The same seed builds the same city.
Collision: `WorldSurface::city` gives the player `City::pushOutOfBuildings` (a circle against the footprints).

## Consequences

- Leblon/Ipanema exist end to end, from the sea bed to the hillside favela above Ipanema, with variety (heights, styles,
  colours, roofs) and the real layout. Other districts (Copacabana, Botafogo, Vidigal's real streets) are a re-run of the two
  scripts with another bounding box.
- Generated geometry is a cache, not source: nothing to migrate on hot reload (it lives in the engine; the game module only
  reads `WorldSurface`).
- Real street names are kept (they are geography); shops, brands and people are invented (the shop signs are blank colour
  bands). OSM data is ODbL: attribution is in `world/ATTRIBUTION.md` and must appear in the game's credits.

## Revision 2026-10-11: streets first, then the ground and the buildings adapt

The first version draped every street on whatever the terrain was and levelled the ground per building. Reviewing it in the game
showed the cost: streets dived into canals, rode bumps, ran beside crater-like cuts, and buildings stood in the carriageway.
The order is now reversed (`tools/district_roads.py`, called from `build_district.py`):

1. **Cross sections fit the buildings.** Each street's asphalt and sidewalks are narrowed to what the real footprints leave it
   (measured on a 1 m raster, left and right separately); footprints still overlapping asphalt are pushed out of it or dropped.
2. **Bridges.** A street that crosses sea, lagoon or canal is cut at the water and the middle piece gets `kEdgeBridge`. The engine
   builds a deck on its own straight grade between the two ends of the edge, with sidewalks, parapets and a slab under it;
   footways and cycle paths on bridges get a parapet and underside too. The ground under a bridge is left alone.
3. **Designed profile.** Every street gets a smoothed height profile from the baked ground (water crossings interpolated over),
   shared at junctions and kept within 4 m of the ground it was designed over.
4. **Stamped into the ground.** The profile is written into the patch, level across the street, blended back to the natural
   ground over a distance that grows with the slope of the hill, so the terrain adapts to the street. The engine drapes the
   asphalt on this ground and no longer has bumps to follow.
5. **Buildings follow the street.** A building's ground floor is the street level in front of it (if that is within 4 m of the
   ground under it; otherwise it stands against the slope). The ground is levelled around it by at most 3.5 m and never inside
   a street's corridor.

Other changes in this revision: "plain or hill" is decided from the erosion of the heightmap (low ground and gentle slope), which
removed a 100 m deep crater on Cantagalo (a bug in how heights above 90 m were treated); paving is kept off steep ground; the
Jardim de Alah canal has a flat 1.4 m bed, concrete walls and a paved quay (`RoadClass::Canal`, edges written by the build
script); cycle paths are smoothed (Chaikin), stop at the kerb and cross the asphalt as flush red paint; streets get a gutter strip,
manholes and storm drains; the paving, asphalt (patches, seams, cracks, oil) and sidewalk (limestone setts) textures are richer;
three parked-car models in Rio's fleet colours along kerbs with room for them, hydrants, round signs, street-name plates at
junction corners, ad panels and newsstands at bus stops.

Known limits of this revision: the cars, signs and panels are low-poly procedural stand-ins; no traffic; the beach avenue and
the promenade are still one flat design (no median, no trees in the cycle path); the Copernicus bias on the hills remains.

## Not done / known limits

- No interiors, no enterable buildings, no balcony geometry (balconies are in the facade texture and its normal map).
- Overhead wires and poles, street signs, road wear decals, traffic and pedestrians are not here.
- Facades are procedural stand-ins: windows read right at street level but have no per-building uniqueness beyond style,
  palette, shop variants and roof; no signage text.
- The Copernicus surface model biases the hills (Cantagalo, Dois Irmaos): platforms follow it; a real terrain model fixes it.
- Overpass is the source: its data changes; the files are rebuilt from the cached JSON (`data/osm`) unless refetched.
- Min-spec performance is not verified (see the measured numbers in the README / ADR 0012 section, dev GPU only).
